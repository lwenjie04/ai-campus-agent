# Product

## Register

product

## Users

The primary users are students who need quick, trustworthy answers for daily campus questions: academic rules, course and exam matters, scholarships, campus life, procedures, student handbook policies, and community-sourced experience.

Students usually arrive with a practical question and limited patience. They need a clear answer first, then supporting sources, follow-up actions, and confidence that official knowledge and community knowledge are not being mixed carelessly.

Secondary users include teachers or administrators who review community content and maintain the knowledge flow, plus evaluators or presentation audiences who need to understand the system's value quickly.

## Product Purpose

This product is a unified campus intelligent service platform combining AI chat, LightRAG-backed student handbook retrieval, source-grounded answers, digital human narration, student community content, and admin review workflows.

Success means students can ask natural-language questions and receive accurate, traceable, easy-to-act-on answers without digging through scattered documents or notices. The interface should also demonstrate the product well in competitions and presentations while remaining stable enough for everyday use.

## Brand Personality

Technological, trustworthy, and clear.

The product should feel like a capable campus AI command center: intelligent and modern, but not cold, childish, or decorative for its own sake. Motion should make the interface feel responsive and alive, with a little presentation energy, while preserving readability and task flow.

## Anti-references

Do not make the interface look like a generic admin template, a childish campus cartoon, or a pile of identical cards. Avoid the current overly soft green-gradient look as the dominant visual language.

Avoid surfaces that feel cluttered, gimmicky, or purely decorative. Glass effects should be restrained and purposeful, not default styling on every panel. The digital human should support the answer experience, not overpower the chat workflow.

## Design Principles

1. Answer first, then prove it.
Students should see the usable answer before citations, metadata, or extra controls. Sources should be visible enough to build trust without slowing the first read.

2. Treat trust as a visual hierarchy.
Official handbook and school knowledge must feel more authoritative than community-sourced content. The UI should make source type and confidence easy to scan.

3. Make the AI feel capable, not theatrical.
Use stronger technology cues, refined motion, and a command-center feel, but keep the experience calm enough for repeated daily use.

4. Unify the whole product surface.
Home chat, login, community, admin review, navigation, source displays, and settings should share one visual system instead of looking like separate pages.

5. Let motion clarify state.
Animations should communicate loading, streaming, listening, speaking, transitions, and review status. Motion can be more expressive than a basic product UI, but it must respect reduced-motion preferences.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA for contrast, keyboard accessibility, focus states, text sizing, and responsive behavior. Motion must support `prefers-reduced-motion` so students sensitive to animation can still use the system comfortably.

The product should handle long Chinese text, mixed official/community source labels, small mobile screens, slow network states, empty states, and error states without text overflow or ambiguous feedback.
